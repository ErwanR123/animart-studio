# Explorer Notebook — Product Specification

## Core idea
The Explorer Notebook is the user's personal reconstruction of culture and history. Every real-world discovery becomes a piece of a larger story. The user does not complete a database; they progressively understand how things connect.

## Model
History → Era → Chapter → Event / Idea / Movement / Person → Artwork / Object / Place → User discovery.

Example: Renaissance → Florence → Humanism → Medici patronage → Botticelli → The Birth of Venus → user sees the painting.

## Discovery philosophy
Transform “I saw this” into “I understand where this belongs.” A discovered work can reveal its period, city, movement, people and surrounding events.

## Personal history
Historical truth is shared; the path through it is personal. Two users may reach Renaissance through different works, cities and museums.

## Chapter structure
Intro, historical context, important people/events/ideas, artworks, places, connections, user discoveries, progress, possible discoveries.

## States
`UNKNOWN → REVEALED → DISCOVERED → EXPLORED → MASTERED`

Unknown may be hidden/faint. Revealed means the user knows it exists. Discovered is connected to a real discovery. Explored means meaningful context was consumed. Mastered is optional and can reflect multiple connections/games. Avoid visible XP grinding.

## Visual representation
Unknown areas create curiosity via faded maps, silhouettes, unfinished timelines or empty sketches. When discovered, content appears, names resolve, connections draw and stamps land. The Notebook should feel like it is filling itself.

## Connections
Works can connect to artist, movement, city, event, political context, mythology, another work, architecture, person or museum. Connections are the core of cross-museum coherence.

## Timeline
Prioritize user discoveries, their context and important connecting events—not thousands of generic dates. The timeline becomes richer as understanding grows.

## World map
Locations become cultural nodes because of what the user learned there/about them. It should answer “Where did the things I've learned about happen?” rather than merely “Where have I traveled?”

## Retroactive discovery
Allow recovery/claiming from previous scans, visit history or manual confirmation. Do not force revisits when the product evolves.

## Scope
Conceptually support painting, sculpture, artifact, architecture, monument, historical place and archaeological site; potentially books/music/scientific objects later. Museums/artworks are the starting point, not the boundary.

## Games
Prefer challenges based on actual discoveries: chronological ordering, movement identification, event/work matching, etc. Games strengthen the user's Notebook.

## Rewards
Unlock meaning: pages, connections, chapter illustrations, badges, stories, Guide annotations, collections, challenges. Avoid coins, gems, loot boxes and arbitrary currency.

## Completion
Culture is infinite. Use milestones rather than mandatory 100% completion. More can always remain to discover.

## Guide
He appears to help build the Notebook: annotate, draw connections, place stamps, reveal pages, point at gaps and suggest exploration.

## Emotional objective
After months: **“Wow, I've actually seen all of this.”** Then: **“There is still so much I don't know.”** Pride + curiosity.

## Avoid Pokémonification
Do not optimize primarily around count, completion percentage, rarity or scarcity. Collection supports understanding, not the opposite.
